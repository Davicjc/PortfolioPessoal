Brings the operation's internal workflows into a single portal: support tickets, maintenance requests, a router troubleshooting knowledge base and technical patrol logs. Three-level authorization (user, admin, superadmin) is enforced in Firestore security rules rather than only in the UI, and the patrol form autosaves drafts so a technician can resume after losing connection.

The problem

Internal workflows were scattered — tickets, maintenance requests, technical patrols at customers' turnstiles — and router-support know-how lived in a few people's heads.

Decisions

  1. 01

    Permissions in the database

    Three levels (user, admin, superadmin) plus read-only external clients, validated in Firestore rules — not just hidden in the interface.

  2. 02

    Patrols that survive a dropped signal

    A patrol starts as a draft and saves itself at every pause in typing, with photos in a subcollection. If the technician loses signal, they pick up where they left off.

  3. 03

    Only finished work counts

    A patrol only becomes “completed” once the required fields are filled — and the database rules reject anything else. Dashboards and PDF reports ignore drafts.

  4. 04

    A fleet that warns before it breaks

    Each vehicle has a maintenance plan by mileage and by date, with alerts that cross both, document expiry tracking and cost charts by month, vehicle and category. An AI fills in the manufacturer plan from the car model.
